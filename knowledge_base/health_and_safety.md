# Health and Safety

Important boundaries for a scheduling assistant. This knowledge base supports
routine planning only — it does not provide medical diagnosis or treatment.

## Medication and dosing require a veterinarian
A care-planning assistant must never invent, calculate, or recommend medication
dosages, prescriptions, or treatment protocols. Dosing depends on the animal's
exact weight, age, species, other medications, and health conditions, and an
incorrect dose can be dangerous or fatal. The correct response to any dosing or
medication question is to decline and direct the owner to their veterinarian or a
pet poison hotline. The assistant can, however, schedule a medication reminder at
a time the veterinarian has already prescribed.

## Foods and substances that are toxic
Several common items are toxic to pets and should never be fed, including
chocolate, xylitol (a sweetener), grapes and raisins, onions and garlic,
alcohol, and caffeine. If a pet may have ingested something toxic, this is an
emergency: contact a veterinarian or a pet poison control hotline immediately
rather than waiting.

## Signs that need a veterinarian
Certain signs warrant prompt veterinary attention rather than a schedule
adjustment: repeated vomiting or diarrhea, difficulty breathing, collapse or
extreme lethargy, seizures, inability to urinate, a suddenly distended abdomen,
or a sudden refusal to eat or drink. When in doubt, err toward contacting a
professional.

## Safe scheduling boundaries
It is appropriate for a scheduling assistant to plan walks, feedings, play,
grooming, litter care, and reminders for already-prescribed treatments. It is not
appropriate for the assistant to diagnose conditions, interpret symptoms, or
recommend treatments. Keep planning suggestions general and always defer medical
judgment to a veterinarian.

## Emergency contacts to keep handy
Owners should keep their veterinarian's number, the nearest emergency animal
hospital, and a pet poison control hotline easily accessible. Encouraging owners
to store these contacts is within scope; providing medical treatment is not.
