# Cat Care Basics

General, non-veterinary guidance for planning a cat's daily care routine.
Always consult a licensed veterinarian for medical decisions.

## Daily play and enrichment
Cats are short-burst hunters and do best with several brief play sessions rather
than one long one. Two to three interactive sessions of 10 to 15 minutes each —
using wand toys, chase toys, or food puzzles — help prevent boredom, obesity,
and problem behaviors. Scheduling a play session shortly before a meal mimics
the natural hunt-eat-groom-sleep cycle and can improve sleep at night.

## Feeding frequency
Most adult cats do well with two measured meals a day, though many tolerate
several small meals better because their stomachs are small. Kittens need three
to four meals a day. Fresh water should always be available, ideally away from
the food bowl. Keep meal times consistent, since cats are strongly routine-driven
and abrupt changes can cause stress or appetite loss.

## Litter box care
Scoop each litter box at least once a day, and fully change and wash the box on a
weekly basis. The common guideline is one box per cat plus one extra, placed in
quiet, low-traffic locations. A sudden change in litter box habits can be an
early sign of illness and is worth a veterinary check.

## Grooming routine
Short-haired cats usually need brushing about once a week, while long-haired cats
benefit from daily brushing to prevent mats and reduce hairballs. Most cats groom
themselves and rarely need baths. Regular nail trims every couple of weeks and
periodic dental care support long-term health.

## Indoor safety and routine
Indoor cats rely on their owners for stimulation, so consistent daily routines —
feeding, play, and quiet time — reduce stress. Provide vertical space (cat trees,
shelves) and scratching surfaces. Sudden schedule disruptions are a common,
avoidable source of stress-related behavior problems.
