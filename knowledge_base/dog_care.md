# Dog Care Basics

General, non-veterinary guidance for planning a dog's daily care routine.
Always consult a licensed veterinarian for medical decisions.

## Daily exercise needs
Most adult dogs need between 30 and 120 minutes of exercise per day, split
across one or more walks or play sessions. High-energy working breeds (border
collies, huskies, retrievers) sit at the top of that range; low-energy or
brachycephalic breeds (bulldogs, pugs) and senior dogs sit near the bottom.
Splitting exercise into a morning and an evening session is generally better
for digestion and behavior than a single long block.

## Puppy exercise limits
Puppies should not be over-exercised while their joints are developing. A common
rule of thumb is about 5 minutes of structured exercise per month of age, up to
twice a day (for example, a 4-month-old puppy gets roughly 20 minutes, twice a
day). Free play in a safe area is fine on top of this. Increase gradually as the
puppy matures.

## Feeding frequency
Adult dogs are usually fed twice a day, spaced roughly 8 to 12 hours apart.
Puppies under six months typically need three to four smaller meals a day to
support growth and keep blood sugar stable. Keep feeding times consistent from
day to day, because a predictable schedule reduces begging and digestive upset.

## Toilet and potty schedule
Adult dogs generally need to relieve themselves 3 to 5 times a day. Puppies need
far more frequent trips outside — roughly every 2 hours, plus right after
waking, eating, and playing. Building fixed potty breaks into the daily plan
(morning, midday, evening, before bed) speeds up house-training.

## Grooming routine
Brushing frequency depends on coat type: short-coated dogs weekly, long or
double-coated dogs several times a week to prevent matting. Baths are usually
needed only once every four to six weeks unless the dog gets dirty, because
over-bathing strips natural oils. Nail trims every three to four weeks and
regular ear checks round out a basic grooming routine.
