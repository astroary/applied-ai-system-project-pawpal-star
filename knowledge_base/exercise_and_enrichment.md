# Exercise and Enrichment

General guidance on physical activity and mental stimulation. Adjust for age,
breed, and any conditions a veterinarian has flagged.

## Why enrichment matters
Physical exercise burns energy, but mental enrichment — sniffing, foraging,
problem-solving — tires a pet in a different and equally important way. A short
enrichment activity (a food puzzle, a sniffing walk, a training game) can reduce
boredom-driven behaviors like chewing, barking, or scratching more effectively
than extra physical exercise alone.

## Splitting activity into sessions
Two shorter activity sessions usually beat one long session. Splitting a dog's
walks into morning and evening, or giving a cat several brief play bursts, keeps
energy balanced through the day and fits more realistically into an owner's
schedule. Aim to attach at least one activity session to the times of day the pet
is naturally most active.

## Weather and timing
In hot weather, schedule walks and vigorous play for the cooler morning and
evening hours and avoid midday heat, which can cause overheating and burned paw
pads on hot pavement. In cold weather, shorter more frequent outings are safer
for small or short-coated pets. Always keep fresh water available around activity.

## Rest and recovery
Pets need substantial rest — many dogs and cats sleep 12 to 16 hours a day.
Building quiet, uninterrupted rest periods into the daily plan is as important as
scheduling activity. Puppies, kittens, and senior pets especially need protected
downtime to recover.
